This is held together by two enabling conditions:

- **Governed & Aligned** — Guided by principles, standards and architecture governance

### What This Structure Enables

Enterprise Architecture with AI as Strategy provides the structure that enables AI solutions to be:

## Operating Models

![Four Operating Models](images/four-operating-models.png)

The core of Enterprise Architecture is the concept of the **Operating Model**, which defines the level of process integration and standardization required to support a company's business strategy.

There are four operating models — **Diversification, Coordination, Replication, and Unification** — each with different requirements for shared processes, data, and systems. Choosing the right operating model is critical, as it guides architectural decisions and aligns technology investments with strategic goals.

### The Four Operating Models

| | **Diversification** | **Coordination** | **Replication** | **Unification** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Style** | Decentralized and flexible | Centralized standards, decentralized execution | Standardized but locally implemented | Centralized and fully integrated |
| **Characteristics** | Highly decentralized · Unique businesses · Local decisions and operations · Minimal standardization | Centralized standards · Decentralized execution · Shared services in key areas · Balance of global and local | Standardized processes · Locally implemented · Consistent approach · Efficiency through replication | Highly centralized · End-to-end integrated · Common processes and data · Maximum standardization |
| **Processes** | Mostly unique | Some standardized | Standardized | Fully standardized |
| **Data** | Mostly local | Shared in key areas | Replicated | Enterprise-wide |
| **Systems** | Mostly local | Shared in key areas | Replicated | Enterprise-wide |
| **AI Approach** | Local AI solutions with greater autonomy, aligned to enterprise principles | Enterprise AI services for shared domains, with local flexibility | Reusable AI models deployed across replicated units | Enterprise-wide AI capabilities, shared and governed centrally |

### Designing AI Capabilities According to the Operating Model

Artificial Intelligence capabilities should be designed according to the chosen operating model. The operating model determines how AI models, data, and decision-making capabilities are shared and governed across the enterprise.

```
MORE LOCAL                                                          MORE INTEGRATED
(Greater Autonomy)                                              (Greater Standardization)
      ◀── Diversification — Coordination — Replication — Unification ──▶
```

### Enterprise-Wide Principles Apply to All Models

Regardless of which operating model is chosen, five principles apply across the board:

| Principle | Description |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | Clear policies and oversight |
| **Ethics** | Responsible and fair AI use |
| **Security** | Protect data, models and systems |
| **Interoperability** | Enable integration and portability |
| **Compliance** | Meet regulatory requirements |

> Choose the right operating model. Design AI capabilities that drive strategic value.

## Foundation for Strategic Execution

![Foundation for Strategic Execution](images/foundation-for-strategic-execution.png)

Enterprise Architecture is the organizing logic for business processes and technology capabilities, reflecting the organization's **Target Operating Model (TOM)**.

Enterprise Architecture supports strategic execution by defining what must be standardized and integrated and what can remain local or flexible. This clarity enables faster decision-making, lower costs, greater agility, and continuous innovation.

### The Four Essential Pillars

A central idea consisting of four essential pillars that enable scalable and consistent execution:

| Pillar | Description |
|---|---|
| **Digitized Core Business Processes** | Standardized, efficient and end-to-end |
| **Trusted Enterprise Data** | Accurate, consistent, governed and accessible |
| **Shared Business Capabilities** | Reusable building blocks that create business value |
| **Reliable Technology Platforms** | Secure, scalable and high-performing infrastructure |

Organizations that establish this foundation can implement new initiatives more rapidly by reusing existing capabilities instead of reinventing them.

### AI Builds on This Foundation

High-quality enterprise data, standardized business processes, and integrated digital platforms are prerequisites for value creation with AI. Enterprise Architecture embeds AI into enterprise capabilities across six areas:

| Capability | Description |
|---|---|
| **Decision Support** | AI-driven insights for better, faster decisions |
| **Process Automation** | Intelligent automation of routine and complex tasks |
| **Predictive Analytics** | Forecast trends and outcomes to drive proactive action |
| **Knowledge Management** | AI-powered search, summarization and knowledge discovery |
| **Customer Engagement** | Personalized experiences across every touchpoint |
| **Operational Optimization** | Continuously optimize performance and resources |

These capabilities draw on four pillars converging into one reusable capability — **AI**: Trusted Enterprise Data, Standardized Business Processes, Integrated Digital Platforms, and Human Expertise and Governance.

> AI becomes a reusable enterprise capability that accelerates innovation while maintaining consistency, transparency, and business alignment.

## Governance & Alignment

![Governance & Alignment](images/governance-and-alignment-cycle.png)

Governance is essential for strategic execution and for sustaining Enterprise Architecture. It establishes the guardrails, decision rights, and accountability needed to align initiatives with enterprise-wide goals and maximize long-term business value.

### Essential Governance Elements

| Element | Description |
|---|---|
| **Strategic Objectives** | Clear enterprise direction and measurable goals guide decisions and investments |
| **Decision Rights** | Defined roles and decision authorities ensure timely, consistent, and effective decisions |
| **Architectural Principles** | Shared principles and standards provide consistency, interoperability, and long-term integrity |
| **Accountability Mechanisms** | Clear ownership, performance metrics, and reporting drive execution and continuous improvement |
| **Investment Governance** | Prioritize and fund initiatives that deliver maximum value and align with strategic objectives |

Effective governance ensures that initiatives align with enterprise-wide goals and value creation, even when business units have competing priorities.

### AI Governance: An Extension of Enterprise Architecture Governance

Organizations must govern not only technology and information, but also the full lifecycle and impact of AI systems. The adoption of AI expands the scope of Enterprise Architecture governance across ten areas:

| Area | Description |
|---|---|
| **AI Models** | Manage and govern models as enterprise assets |
| **Training Data** | Ensure data quality, provenance, and representativeness |
| **Model Lifecycle Management** | Oversee development, deployment, monitoring, and retirement |
| **Transparency & Explainability** | Ensure AI decisions are understandable and explainable |
| **Security & Privacy** | Protect data, models, and outputs across the lifecycle |
| **Regulatory Compliance** | Meet applicable laws, standards, and industry requirements |
| **Ethical Principles** | Build and use AI responsibly, fairly, and inclusively |
| **Human Oversight & Accountability** | Maintain human judgment, oversight, and ultimate accountability |
| **Risk Management** | Identify, assess, and mitigate AI-specific risks proactively |
| **Interoperability & Integration** | Ensure AI capabilities integrate securely with enterprise systems |

> AI governance ensures AI systems remain trustworthy, accountable, secure, and aligned with organizational strategy and values.

### A Continuous Governance Cycle

| Direct | Decide | Execute | Monitor | Improve |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Set strategy, principles, and priorities | Evaluate options, make value-based decisions | Implement with standards, guidance, and oversight | Track performance, compliance, and value realization | Learn, adapt, and continuously enhance performance |

**Outcomes:** Aligned initiatives · Better decisions · Lower risk · Higher trust · Sustainable value

> Strong governance. Smarter decisions. Trusted AI. Sustainable value.
> Governance and alignment turn strategy into results — with AI as a trusted partner.

## Summary

Enterprise Architecture with AI as Strategy keeps the discipline's core promise intact — a shared logic connecting business strategy to execution — while extending it in four ways: the **operating model** determines how centralized or local AI capabilities should be; a **foundation** of trusted data, standardized processes, and reliable platforms makes AI value creation possible; and **governance** extends beyond technology and information to cover the full lifecycle of AI systems. The result is the same strategic blueprint organizations have always relied on, now intelligent, adaptive, and AI-powered.
